FAQ

Why does Entropy-Compatible Hiring exist?

The Operational Entropy Index identifies recurring conditions that make organizations harder to operate: knowledge that doesn't travel, workflows that stall, tools that create friction, handoffs that lose information, and companies that remain unnecessarily dependent on specific people.

Any changes that are implemented in an ops overhaul of any kind, are simply a checkpoint. Entropy will inevitably begin creeping in depending on people's behavior, so a specific issue stands out: what happens when new people are hired and they have behaviors that increase the once-mitigated entropy in the newly established system? What happens if we figure out a way to do the opposite, and help maintain the checkpoint?

That's why ECH exists. It gives companies a structured way to observe those behaviors during hiring instead of hoping they'll become apparent through an ordinary interview.

The emphasis on structured observation is deliberate. Structured interviews use predefined rules for eliciting and evaluating evidence.

What does an ECH archetype actually represent?

An archetype is not a personality type. It represents a recognizable pattern of behaviors that tends to protect a company against particular sources of operational entropy.

  • A Documenter, for example, isn't simply “someone who likes documentation.” The archetype represents a combination of behaviors that becomes especially useful when knowledge is trapped in people's heads or difficult for others to retrieve and use.
  • A Clarity Creator represents behaviors that become valuable when information routinely loses meaning as it moves between people or teams.

The archetype is therefore best understood as an operational contribution pattern. Meaning, when X kind of entropy appears, this person naturally tends to behave in ways that help counteract it.

Why do the archetypes exist?

Two excellent employees can respond very differently to the same dysfunctional system.

One person may instinctively document what everyone else keeps explaining verbally, while another may notice that ownership disappears during handoffs. Another yet may immediately see that three tools and four manual steps are standing between a request and its completion.

Those differences matter when you already know what kinds of operational friction your organization experiences. ECH turns those recurring patterns into something that can be deliberately observed during an interview.

The archetypes give names and structure to those patterns so companies can test for relevant behaviors instead of relying on impressions like “they seem organized” or “I think they'd work well here.”

Why these archetypes?

They weren't chosen as a general taxonomy of people.

They were derived from the five dimensions of the Operational Entropy Index:

  1. Founder Dependency
  2. Knowledge Logistics
  3. Workflow Velocity
  4. Tool Discipline
  5. Handoff Integrity

ECH looks at the places where those dimensions interact and asks which recurring behavioral pattern would be particularly useful under those operational conditions. So, the resulting archetypes represent specific combinations of entropy pressures.

That's why there are ten, and why ECH doesn't attempt to describe every useful kind of employee or every way a human can contribute to an organization.

It describes the patterns relevant to the operational problems OEI measures.

How does Entropy-Compatible Hiring relate to the OEI?

The OEI diagnoses the system, while ECH examines what kinds of human behavior would help that system resist the entropy it's currently producing once structural changes have mitigated the main entropy drivers.

An OEI assessment might reveal, for example, that a company has significant Knowledge Logistics and Handoff Integrity problems. ECH can then identify the archetype associated with that combination and provide an interview instrument designed to surface the relevant behaviors.

Operational conditions lead us to a relevant archetype, which surfaces a structured exercise, which leads to observed evidence in the interview process.

You don't need to have completed a formal OEI engagement to use ECH. The application includes the framework necessary to select and use the appropriate instruments.

Does ECH determine who is the best candidate for the role?

No, and it's not supposed to.

ECH is deliberately role-agnostic.

It doesn't determine whether someone is the best engineer, accountant, salesperson, nor executive.

Your company still needs to evaluate the things specific to the actual role:

  • technical or professional competence
  • required knowledge
  • relevant experience
  • work samples or skill assessments
  • role-specific requirements

ECH evaluates how each person appears to behave when confronted with operational conditions associated with particular forms of entropy.

  • Someone can perform extremely well in an ECH instrument and still lack the skills required for the job.
  • Someone can possess exceptional role-specific skills while showing little evidence of the operational behavior you're testing for.
  • Those are different pieces of a hiring decision, and ECH intentionally keeps them separate.

Can someone not have an archetype?

Absolutely. The ECH archetypes are not intended to classify every person.

They were mapped specifically to the operational dimensions measured by OEI. Human beings interact with organizations in far more ways than those five dimensions can or should describe.

Someone may therefore show no particularly strong alignment with an ECH archetype, and that is not a failure, deficiency, nor a negative result. It simply means that their most characteristic contributions aren't well described by this particular framework.

ECH is a map of certain operationally useful behavioral patterns, but it's not a map of human value.

Can someone fit more than one archetype?

Yes.

Archetypes describe behavioral patterns, not mutually exclusive identities. A person may demonstrate behaviors associated with several archetypes, especially because the underlying OEI dimensions interact with one another. Which means that the purpose isn't to discover someone's "One True Type". What you must ask is if the candidate exhibits the behaviors relevant to the operational conditions you're hiring them into.

Is this a personality test?

No.

ECH doesn't ask candidates to describe what kind of person they believe themselves to be and then assign them a type. The interview instruments place candidates into applied situations designed around the operational conditions represented by an archetype. Interviewers observe what candidates notice, ask, challenge, clarify, prioritize, document, or change.

The emphasis is on behavioral evidence gathered during structured exercises, not self-description.

Why not just ask behavioral interview questions?

ECH does include behavioral questions, but they're only one source of evidence.

There's an important difference between asking: “Tell me about a time you improved a difficult handoff.”, and giving someone an imperfect handoff and observing what they actually notice and do with it.

ECH combines direct questions with applied exercises and predefined observable signals. That produces multiple opportunities for the relevant behavior to appear.

It also gives the interviewer something more specific to record than a general impression of whether the answer was “good.”

Does ECH score candidates?

Not in the conventional sense. ECH was intentionally designed around evidence rather than arbitrary numerical candidate scores.

During an interview, evidence can support an expected behavioral signal, contradict it, or simply be insufficient to reach a conclusion.

ECH preserves that uncertainty instead of turning every incomplete observation into pre-analysis.

Why customize the exercises to each company?

Because operational behavior only makes sense in context. A generic exercise can tell you something about how a candidate approaches a hypothetical problem.

ECH can instead use the company's actual conditions to construct an exercise that resembles the environment the candidate would actually be entering.

The surface scenario changes, but the methodology doesn't.

Each archetype defines what the exercise must test, which conditions must be present, what behaviors should be observed, and how the evidence should be interpreted. The software combines those fixed methodological requirements with company-specific context to create the exercise.

Release notes Version 0.1.0 BETA

Initial Windows desktop release of the OEI Entropy-Compatible Hiring interview instrument.

Included

  • Ten OEI archetype-specific interview instruments.
  • Optional routing from two known OEI entropy drivers to one recommended instrument.
  • Packet-specific company-context intake.
  • Progressive optional fields for more detailed customization.
  • AI-assisted exercise generation using the customer’s own OpenAI API key.
  • Clearly labeled deterministic fallback when AI isn’t available.
  • Mandatory interviewer review and approval before candidate release.
  • Separate candidate-safe and private interviewer views.
  • Evidence states:
    • Evidence observed
    • Contrary evidence observed
    • Insufficient evidence
  • Section-level note capture with consolidated notes.
  • Evidence provenance carried into the final report.
  • Structured OEI evidence reports with explicit assessment boundaries.
  • Candidate-link resending, case restart, and case conclusion controls.
  • OEI-branded desktop interface.
  • Four-slide desktop landing presentation with keyboard navigation.

Desktop delivery

  • Distributed as a single Windows installer EXE.
  • Runs a private, loopback-only application service.
  • Customers don’t need Node.js, repository files, or supporting folders.
  • Candidate material opens in a separate candidate-safe window.
  • The recommended Beta workflow is to screen-share only the candidate-safe window during a video call.

AI and API keys

  • No shared API key is embedded in the application.
  • Customers can provide their own OpenAI API key.
  • Keys remain session-only and aren’t intentionally written to disk.
  • Company context is sent to OpenAI only when AI-assisted generation is used.
  • The methodology, evidence categories, and interpretation rules remain deterministic and packet-defined.

Beta limitations

  • Candidate links aren’t remotely hosted or independently shareable yet.
  • Candidate delivery currently relies on screen-sharing the candidate-safe window.
  • Interview cases and candidate sessions aren’t persistently stored after the application closes.
  • The installer isn’t commercially code-signed, so Windows may display a SmartScreen warning.
  • Deterministic fallback exercises are below the intended AI-assisted quality and are clearly labeled.
  • Windows is the only packaged platform in this release.

Assessment boundary

This software records operational-behavior evidence relevant to specific OEI archetypes and pillars. It doesn’t determine overall employee quality or replace evaluation of skills, experience, technical competence, references, or broader hiring fit.
