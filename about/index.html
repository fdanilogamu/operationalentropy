OEI Institute

Operational Forensics for Growing Teams

The OEI Institute governs the Operational Entropy Index methodology and its practice standards. It equips growing organizations to understand and address the operational strain that emerges as they grow.

What the Institute exists to do

As organizations grow, operational strain naturally emerges. Knowledge becomes harder to preserve and retrieve, decisions become more distributed, and handoffs more fragile. Workflows accumulate friction, tools and processes drift, and key people can remain hidden dependencies.

The Institute exists to help organizations investigate and address these conditions before they become accepted as the unavoidable cost of growth.

Operational Forensics

Operational forensics is the evidence-based study of how work actually functions. Evidence from people, systems, records, and workflows helps reconstruct how an organization operates.

The discipline separates observation from interpretation and tests explanations for the structural conditions shaping work. It preserves uncertainty where evidence is incomplete and produces defensible findings that growing teams can act on.

The Operational Entropy Index

The Operational Entropy Index is the core methodology governed by the Institute. It provides a structured way to examine operational entropy across five pillars: Founder Dependency, Knowledge Logistics, Workflow Velocity, Tool Discipline, and Handoff Integrity.

These pillars organize investigation and analysis within the standards maintained by the Institute.

Maintaining the Methodology and Its Standards

The Institute maintains the OEI canon: the established definitions, principles, standards, and practice guidance that determine what counts as OEI. It covers evidence, analysis and synthesis, measurement and scoring, and practitioner expectations.

The Institute distinguishes established doctrine and guidance from work still in development. Deliberate revision allows the methodology to evolve while protecting against drift as more people practice it.

Developing Practitioners

The Institute is developing training, competency standards, an assessment environment, and a certification structure for responsible OEI practice.

Certification is being built around demonstrated competence in scoping, evidence gathering, analysis, reasoning under uncertainty, findings, recommendations, client communication, and methodological stewardship. The practitioner and certification system is still being developed.

Documentation, Tools and Applied Systems

Repeatable OEI practice requires documented knowledge and shared systems. The Institute maintains practitioner documentation, structured workflows, diagnostic tools, and software for evidence capture, analysis, assessment, and application.

This infrastructure makes founder and practitioner knowledge available for consistent application. It also includes products derived from OEI concepts, such as Entropy Compatible Hiring.
