Pricing

Practitioner development offers three distinct pathways, each with a defined practice authorization.

Each pathway uses the Institute’s competency-based certification assessment. Training and assessment are paid first; the authorization payment is due after passing. Payment does not guarantee certification.

More about training, assessment, and authorization pathways

Practitioner development

Training and authorization prices

Training develops capability. The assessment determines whether certification is earned. Authorization, granted only after passing and completing the applicable payment, sets the practitioner’s permitted scope and capacity.

Company-sponsored trainee

$10,000 USD total

Build internal OEI capability within the sponsoring organization.

$6,000 training and assessment
$4,000 authorization, payable after passing

May conduct full OEI engagements and Focused Investigations within the sponsoring organization. This authorization does not permit outside client work or Institute representation. The employee retains certification. Authorization is held by the practitioner and scoped to the sponsoring organization.

Annual renewal: $500 per employee, per year. The company pays while the practitioner remains on staff.

Independent consultant trainee

$12,000 USD total

Develop an independent practice serving multiple clients; the practitioner acquires their own clients.

$7,200 training and assessment
$4,800 authorization, payable after passing

May identify OEI as the methodology used and the OEI Institute as its source. May conduct full OEI engagements and Focused Investigations for clients, but may not represent the Institute or imply it is responsible for independent engagements. Independent full-engagement fees are capped at 50% of the corresponding Native fee: $10,000–$22,500+ based on current Native pricing.

Annual renewal: $600 per year.

OEI Native practitioner

$15,000 USD total

Qualify for authorization to represent the Institute and deliver engagements to Institute clients under its standards and delivery arrangements.

$9,000 training and assessment
$6,000 authorization, payable after passing

Includes training and the opportunity to qualify for authorization. Passing is required; payment does not guarantee certification, Native status, or a client assignment.

Annual renewal: $750 per year.

Annual authorization renewal

Renewal is 5% of the applicable pathway price. The first year of renewal is waived after certification. Renewal sustains active authorization and supports alignment with the current OEI methodology through Institute-provided Methodology Updates.

Company-sponsored renewal is $500 per employee, not per company. If a practitioner upgrades pathways, the renewal rate changes immediately to 5% of the new pathway price. For example, company-sponsored authorization changes from $500 to $600 per year after an independent upgrade, or to $750 per year after an OEI Native upgrade. Read the renewal, lapse, and update rules.

Upgrading from company-sponsored authorization

If a practitioner leaves the sponsoring organization, the company stops paying renewal for that employee and their company-specific authorization ends. The practitioner retains their certification. They may upgrade to independent authorization for $2,000, or pursue the OEI Native pathway for $7,500, subject to applicable certification and authorization requirements, or allow authorization to lapse.

How AI Enablement is scoped ↓

A bounded entry point

Focused Investigations

Investigate a specific operational domain through evidence-first OEI inquiry. Each investigation is scoped to the question and the work required; these are not identical, fixed-scope packages.

Current public range

Optional intervention layer

AI Enablement — scoped from OEI findings

AI Enablement can be added to either OEI pathway when the investigation identifies a worthwhile opportunity. It is scoped according to the intervention the OEI work actually supports; it is not an automatic part of every engagement.

OEI begins with evidence, determines whether AI is appropriate, and tests any intervention against operational outcomes. The right conclusion may be not to proceed with AI.

Explore AI Enablement

AI is not an OEI pillar

It is an optional application layer, considered only when OEI findings justify it.
