Understanding OEI

What is the Operational Entropy Index?

The OEI is a structured methodology for examining operational strain as an organization grows. It uses evidence about how work moves to identify where friction accumulates and what conditions affect execution.

What does the OEI examine?

The OEI examines five connected dimensions: Founder Dependency, Knowledge Logistics, Workflow Velocity, Tool Discipline, and Handoff Integrity.

How does an OEI investigation gather evidence?

OEI investigations can use structured interviews, workflow mapping, operational testing, and root-cause mapping. The aim is to compare how work is intended to happen with how it actually happens.

Does a client complaint prove that operational entropy is the cause?

No. A complaint is a signal to investigate, not proof of a particular cause. The same client experience can have multiple causes, so it should be examined alongside evidence about workflows, ownership, and system records.

Choosing an OEI pathway

How long can a full OEI engagement take?

The longest option combines a four-day OEI Diagnosis, a 30-day Audit, and a 90-day multi-pain-point sprint.

Should I start with a broad diagnosis or a Focused Investigation?

A Focused Investigation fits when the problem is already visible and bounded, such as a founder bottleneck, trapped knowledge, stalled work, tool complexity, or failing handoffs. If there is operational friction but its source is unclear, start with an OEI Diagnosis.

What is included in an OEI Diagnosis, and what does it cost?

The OEI Diagnosis is a four-day data intake and analysis. As a standalone engagement, it typically costs $2,000 USD. Organizations can choose it on its own to see what the analysis finds.

Can my organization build its own OEI capability, or do practitioners need to do the work?

Organizations can develop internal capability through training and certification, or have qualified OEI practitioners perform the diagnosis and methodology application. The Institute is developing its practitioner development pathway.

What qualifies someone as an OEI Practitioner?

An OEI Practitioner must pass a certification exam proctored by an OEI Practitioner.

How much do OEI services cost?

Current public ranges are $1,000–$4,000 USD for Focused Investigations, $8,000–$15,000+ USD for internal OEI Practitioner Development, and $20,000–$45,000+ USD for practitioner-led OEI engagements. Final scope and pricing depend on the organization and work involved.

Are Focused Investigations fixed packages?

No. Each investigation is scoped to the operational question and the work required. Effort varies with the judgment, evidence gathering, and analysis involved.

Can a Focused Investigation lead to broader OEI work?

Yes. If a Focused Investigation reveals a wider structural condition, the work can expand into a broader OEI diagnosis, audit, or intervention.

Discovery calls and OEI work

How long is a discovery call?

The calendar provides a short initial conversation to understand the situation and decide whether further discussion is useful.

Is there any cost for a discovery call?

The initial conversation is exploratory. Any later scope or commercial terms are discussed separately.

How quickly can we start?

Timing depends on the question, available evidence, and the delivery structure that applies to the work.

What timezone are you in?

The calendar shows current availability.

Can OEI engagements be done remotely?

Most engagements are conducted remotely. If an engagement requires in-person involvement, travel expenses must be considered.

What should I prepare for the discovery call?

Nothing special. Be ready to talk about where execution is breaking down and what brought you to the OEI. Once the first engagement is scheduled, you’ll receive a list of the minimum requirements needed from your company.

What happens after the discovery call?

If an engagement is appropriate, the next steps are to schedule its first part, sign the agreement, and provide the company with the minimum requirements list. The first part is usually the four-day Diagnosis, beginning with a founder intake.

When is payment due?

The payment window opens when the first engagement is scheduled. Payment is due by 11:59 p.m. GMT-5 on the scheduled engagement initialization date.

Are software and travel expenses included?

Basic software, especially OEI-made software, is included. If the engagement requires a specific software license that OEI does not have, the client covers that license. Travel expenses are not included.

Is this worth the investment?

The value of OEI depends on the operational question, the evidence needed to answer it, and the scope of work. Current public pricing ranges are listed on the Pricing page.

How is OEI different from hiring a consultant or operations person?

OEI is a governed methodology for examining how work actually functions and identifying the structural conditions that shape execution. It can be applied through focused investigations, practitioner-led engagements, or internal capability development.

What if nothing changes?

The appropriate form of follow-up depends on the agreed scope. Any findings, recommendations, or applied work should be clear about what was examined and what remains uncertain.

How do I know you can help us?

The initial conversation helps determine whether the OEI methodology is relevant to the situation and whether the current delivery options are suitable.

What if our situation is too complex?

Complexity is usually a symptom of entropy. Usually when things feel complex, it's because systems aren't clear or information isn't flowing right. That's exactly what the OEI is designed to untangle.

AI Enablement

Do I need to use AI as part of OEI work?

No. AI Enablement is optional. OEI work first examines the operating condition. Findings may support an AI intervention, another operational change, or a decision not to use AI.

What happens during AI Enablement Through OEI?

The work investigates a bounded operating condition, establishes a baseline, and designs and tests a governed intervention if AI appears appropriate. The result may be a working capability or an implementation-ready design, along with an evidence-based decision to scale, modify, stop, or expand the investigation.

Entropy Compatible Hiring

What do I get when I buy ECH?

ECH is a Windows desktop application with ten OEI-derived archetype instruments and separate candidate hiring and existing-employee contribution mapping modules. Version 0.1.3 is offered for a one-time beta price of $950–$1,450 USD, with no subscription.

How is ECH delivered after purchase?

The software is sent to the email address requested in the PayPal form.

What is the difference between the ECH purchase options?

The $950 USD option provides the software as-is. The $1,450 USD option provides the software as-is and includes access to future updates and improvements.

Where is ECH data stored?

ECH data stays on the machine where the software is being used. It is not stored between sessions.

Can ECH be used outside the United States?

Yes. ECH can be used anywhere. The application is currently available in English only.

Do I need an OEI diagnosis to use ECH?

No. You don't need to have completed a formal OEI engagement to use ECH. The application includes the framework necessary to select and use the appropriate instruments.

Why not just do a regular interview?

Regular interviews help you evaluate whether someone can do the job, regardless of the environment they'll inevitably influence with their presence and interaction.

ECH helps you evaluate how they’ll operate inside the system that already exists inside the target company.

Candidates complete a role-agnostic exercise tied to the entropy patterns you need the hire to counter, giving the interviewer observable evidence instead of another hypothetical answer. It complements the interview and gives you something concrete to evaluate.

Can ECH be used with existing employees?

Yes. Version 0.1.3 includes Existing Employee Contribution Mapping as a separate module. It compares employee reflection with manager observation and recommends an archetype for applied validation.

The result is an exploratory contribution hypothesis, not a performance rating or permanent label. Hiring and existing-employee sessions remain separate.

Why does Entropy-Compatible Hiring exist?

The OEI identifies conditions that make organizations harder to operate: trapped knowledge, stalled workflows, tool friction, damaged handoffs, and dependency on specific people.

Operational improvements are checkpoints. New hires can either reintroduce entropy or help resist it.

ECH uses structured exercises and predefined evaluation rules to make those behaviors observable during hiring.

What does an ECH archetype represent?

An archetype is an operational contribution pattern, not a personality type. It describes behaviors that help counter a particular source of entropy.

  • A Documenter helps when knowledge is trapped or difficult to retrieve.
  • A Clarity Creator helps when information loses meaning between people or teams.

Why do the archetypes exist?

Strong employees can respond differently to the same dysfunctional system. One may document verbal knowledge, another may clarify ownership, and another may remove unnecessary tools or steps.

Archetypes make those patterns testable, replacing impressions such as “they seem organized” with observable behavior.

Why these archetypes?

They come from the five OEI dimensions:

  1. Founder Dependency
  2. Knowledge Logistics
  3. Workflow Velocity
  4. Tool Discipline
  5. Handoff Integrity

The ten archetypes represent useful behavioral patterns where those dimensions interact. They cover the operational problems OEI measures, not every way a person can contribute.

How does ECH relate to the OEI?

The Operational Entropy Index methodology examines the system. ECH identifies behaviors that can help an improved system resist renewed entropy.

For example, Knowledge Logistics and Handoff Integrity problems point to a relevant archetype and its structured interview exercise. The exercise then produces observable evidence.

Does ECH determine who is the best candidate for the role?

No. ECH is role-agnostic and evaluates behavior under specific operational conditions.

You must separately evaluate competence, knowledge, experience, work samples, and other role requirements.

A candidate can perform well in an ECH exercise but lack role skills, or have strong role skills without showing the behavior being tested. Both are separate parts of the hiring decision.

Can someone have no archetype match?

Absolutely. ECH covers behavioral patterns tied to five OEI dimensions, not every human contribution.

No strong alignment is not a failure or negative result. It only means this framework does not describe the person's most characteristic contributions.

Can someone fit more than one archetype?

Yes. Archetypes are behavioral patterns, not exclusive identities. A person may show several because OEI dimensions interact.

The relevant question is whether the candidate shows the behaviors needed for the operational conditions they will enter.

Is ECH a personality test?

No. ECH does not classify candidates from self-description.

It uses applied situations based on operational conditions. Interviewers observe what candidates notice, question, clarify, prioritize, document, or change.

Why not just ask behavioral interview questions?

Describing a past handoff is different from receiving an imperfect one and showing what you notice and do.

ECH combines questions, applied exercises, and predefined signals so interviewers can record specific evidence instead of a general impression.

Does ECH score candidates?

No. ECH records evidence rather than numerical scores.

Evidence may support a signal, contradict it, or be insufficient. The report preserves that uncertainty.

Why customize the exercises to each company?

Behavior is easier to evaluate in a realistic context. ECH uses company conditions to create a scenario resembling the environment the candidate will enter.

The scenario changes, but the methodology does not. Each archetype retains fixed test conditions, observable behaviors, and interpretation rules.
