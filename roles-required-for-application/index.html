OEI Roles Required for Application

Research makes operational reality legible. The Practitioner determines what it means and what should change.

Applying the Operational Entropy Index involves two complementary roles: Researcher and Practitioner. They hold different responsibilities and use distinct competency models. They are not interchangeable titles or simply levels of seniority.

An OEI engagement requires both research and analysis, but the work does not always have to be performed by two different people. Whether one person may perform both roles depends on that person’s role-specific training, certification, and authorization.

01 · Field research

The Researcher

The Researcher makes operating reality legible. They carry out assigned field research, collect and qualify evidence, and leave a traceable record that a Practitioner can analyze.

Conduct and observe

Conduct assigned interviews and research activities. Observe actual workflows, decisions, tools, knowledge use, and handoffs. Notice potentially relevant patterns without presenting them as established diagnoses.

Capture and validate

Record evidence with its source, context, and limitations. Validate claims using assigned samples, artifacts, and measurements. Preserve missing evidence, contradictions, uncertainty, and unresolved questions.

Prepare the research record

Maintain the OEI Admin Panel as an analysis-ready record. Hand off what was observed, reported, measured, validated, contradicted, and left unresolved without replacing the record with an unsupported diagnostic narrative.

Researcher competency model

The Researcher Competency Model defines six role-specific competencies across three domains:

Field Research

  • Methodical Research Execution
  • Operational Observation & Framework Literacy

Evidence Quality

  • Evidence Capture & Source Integrity
  • Validation, Sampling & Measurement Preparation
  • Uncertainty, Contradiction & Escalation Management

Research Record & Transition

  • Analysis-Ready Recordkeeping

Role boundary: The Researcher may observe, ask, examine, collect, validate, record, notice patterns, flag, and escalate. They do not independently assign final OEI scores or diagnoses, declare final drivers or root causes, resolve analytical ambiguity beyond assigned procedures, prescribe recommendations, or design interventions.

Researcher certification is a complete qualification for the research role. It is not partial Practitioner certification. A Researcher is authorized to perform the role’s research functions and hand off the record to an authorized Practitioner. Researcher qualification does not authorize the person to complete the OEI process independently.

Company-Sponsored Researcher candidates have one assessment attempt, with no retake or repeat training. This role produces the evidence a Practitioner interprets, so the standard is set where the record can be trusted. A candidate who does not meet it after role-specific training is unlikely to meet it on a further attempt, which is why a replacement means a new candidate.

02 · Analysis and delivery

The Practitioner

The Practitioner interprets the research record and applies OEI analysis. This role owns the analytical judgments and guides the diagnosis through client-facing delivery.

Evaluate and analyze

Work with evidence prepared by the Researcher. Judge whether it is sufficient for the intended analysis, examine the record as a whole, and develop defensible interpretations rather than treating individual observations as diagnoses.

Confirm and diagnose

Review and confirm metric inputs, interpret findings, preserve uncertainty and limitations, and make diagnostic judgments supported by the evidence.

Recommend and deliver

Develop recommendations and intervention designs where appropriate, prioritize the work, and lead the client-facing interpretation and delivery of the engagement.

The Practitioner Competency Model defines twelve competencies in five domains. They cover investigation design and evidence discipline, analytical reasoning, OEI application, professional delivery, and methodological stewardship. Research is not optional to rigorous OEI work. The Practitioner remains accountable for evidence sufficiency and the conclusions drawn from it.

Scoring is calculated from confirmed metric inputs under the methodology. The Practitioner confirms and interprets those inputs and is responsible for the diagnosis. The Practitioner Competency Model and methodology define the scope of this role.

Assessment: The Practitioner assessment allows a second attempt. The work turns on judgment and analysis rather than a fixed condition, so a different approach can change the result.

03 · A disciplined handoff

How the roles work together

Researcher

Produce a qualified record

The Researcher creates a traceable, contextualized, analysis-ready account of the evidence and its limits.

Practitioner

Determine what the evidence supports

The practitioner interprets the complete record and decides what can responsibly be concluded.

The practitioner may request clarification or additional research when evidence is incomplete, a source is unclear, a contradiction needs investigation, or a sample is insufficient. Returning a question to research protects the analysis from unsupported certainty.

This handoff preserves a distinction between evidence collection and analytical judgment. It does not make the Researcher an administrative assistant or reduce the Practitioner to a recipient of a finished answer.

04 · Pathways and role coverage

Who needs which role?

The Institute’s pathways reflect how an organization or practitioner staffs the two functions. Role-specific training and qualification determine who may perform each function.

Organization building internal capability

The $10,000 Company-Sponsored pathway includes one Practitioner training and assessment process and one separate Researcher training and assessment process for two different people, plus one Practitioner certification and authorization. The Researcher works with the Practitioner by preparing and handing off the analysis-ready evidence record. The Company-Sponsored Researcher candidate has one assessment attempt and cannot retake or repeat training.

If the Researcher candidate fails but the Practitioner passes, the $4,000 Practitioner authorization payment is still due. The company then has an authorized Practitioner without a qualified Researcher. It may train a replacement Researcher candidate for $3,000. The Practitioner’s authorization applies within the sponsoring organization. Researcher qualification permits research functions in support of that Practitioner. Neither person may use this pathway to serve external clients or represent the Institute. See the replacement Researcher terms.

Independent consultant

The independent pathway trains one individual for both roles. That person may perform both research and practitioner functions only after meeting the requirements and holding the relevant role certifications and authorization. The individual is responsible for their own clients and works within the independent authorization scope.

OEI Native

The OEI Native pathway qualifies an individual to deliver engagements on behalf of the Institute and represent it, subject to assessment and authorization. It does not assume Researcher training is included. The Institute may train or assign Researchers separately.
